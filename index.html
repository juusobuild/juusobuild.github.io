Strain Gauges

I watched a YouTube video about strain gauges. The things I learned were:

  • Strain gauges measure resistance. If you stretch the gauge, the resistance increases.
  • Shorter gauge length means more accuracy, but longer gauge length is often more practical.
  • Constantan is a widely used material in strain gauges with a gauge factor around 2.0.
  • Typical strain measurement is 1–1000 με and electrical resistance is commonly 120Ω or 350Ω.
  • A Wheatstone bridge is used to determine the electrical resistance of the strain gauge.
  • Temperature compensation methods include:
    • Active compensation using a dummy strain gauge in an unloaded area
    • Self-compensation using a material with the same coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE)
  • A strain gauge rosette uses three strain gauges, one placed at a 45° angle.
